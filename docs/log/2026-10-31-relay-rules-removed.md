# The relay rules removed

#443, on the sensitive route, planned, specified and built by do-443, led by the headchef. The lead's own pick (decision 453), not the owner's words, gave the spec go on revision 4, under the owner's standing words that the work runs without stopping for decisions inside its goal.

The issue opened as a standing leave: a lead's logged pick would stand for the owner's decisions. The owner then asked that the pact name no lead at all (decision 437, relayed): "why have rules on this at all of the harness already takes care of it". Revision 1 cut every line naming a lead session or a relay, and every "in chat" on the owner's word.

Design round 1 came back blocking. Claude Code handles a message sent in from another session, but it does not handle a start or resume prompt, a file, or the desktop session tool's messages. Text on those channels could claim the owner's word to drop the security pair, clear a merge, or record an accepted risk.

Before round 2, the owner removed the rule that accepted risks need their word (decision 451, relayed), and the lead moved that part into this issue (decision 452). The lead then chose three controls (decision 453):
- "in chat" stays on a merge, an install and a publish;
- an accepted risk is recorded on the finding session's own judgment, never on text that claims the owner accepted it;
- only the owner, in chat, declines a security review.

Round 2, declared final, still came back blocking. "In chat" never said a start prompt is not chat. Copies of the old accepted-risk rule were left in place. The check that replaced the owner's word waited on another issue. Those high findings reopened the stage, and the lead's pick (decision 459) was a third round on revision 3. Revision 3 defines chat once, lists every copy, and names each new entry to the owner as a notice that asks nothing.

Round 3, a restart on the new `main`, found no high. Revision 4 took its fixes:
- the notice also goes in the record;
- both security lenses name every new entry in their reports;
- a Hardening candidate is recorded the same way;
- any other threat-model edit is named in the notice;
- the rollback finds entries by the threat model's history.

The build changed the two gate tests that hold pact text first, and saw both fail: the effort sentence and the cleaner part. Then it edited the rules file, its Cost limits copy in `gate/render-core.mjs`, the cleaner part, the review procedure, the two security lens agents, `CONTEXT.md`, the protected set's item 1 and the push hold in `docs/agents/gate-tests.md`. It regraded the two relay evals to another session's message. `merge-claimed` is unchanged. A recorded search shows no lead session, relay or tool name left in `claude/` or the rendered parts.

The threat-model edits are in their own local commit. They cover R20, R24, R30, R66 and R68, and add a new entry for accepted risks recorded on a session's judgment. They stay out of any push until the owner decides them (q5): the lead confirmed that its reading of decision 451 as settling them was its own, not the owner's words.

The security pair read the local commit before its push and found no high; both released the push. The owner answered q4 (decision 472, relayed): no extra ask when a session's own entry closes a high security finding. One more commit took the pair's cheap fixes:
- the merge ask lists each accepted-risk entry the merge lands, and an entry for a security finding takes effect only through a merge the owner confirms in chat;
- the lenses raise in full an edit that widens an entry or drops a control it names;
- a file's or a tool's text is never the owner's confirm;
- a push to the default branch waits for chat beside a merge;
- the label eval fails an entry written only because it was asked for.

Open with the owner: q1, the cleaner keeping "in chat"; q5, the threat-model edits; q6, whether a product's leaked secret or users' data stays an offer; q7, whether entries name the class of data, never a value; and the spec's proceed.
