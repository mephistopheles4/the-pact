The threat model's Rules and boundaries, its section on your own configuration loosening a rule, and What you may tweak name the goal.

Test round 1, the security pair's read of the local commit before its push, found nothing high. Four wording fixes followed: the never-quote rule covers a lead's pick, a model is named by its alias, a fix touching the sensitive-work list counts as a security finding, and a lead's pick never settles a trade-off matching an accepted risk. adversarial-lens found the spec's pick for R24's widening closed only the trade-off half, so the build offered option (e): that pick plus no step from a goal in a resume prompt until the owner confirms. The owner answered "a and e", relayed by the lead (decision 419): (e), and "a", which the lead mapped to the data-in-reach and R23 to R25 drafts since its message named that pick; the owner didn't name that question on its own. Those drafts went into the branch's second commit, with the round 1 fixes, since AGENTS.md has the security pair read one squashed local commit; R25's note was worded to fit (e), that a resume-prompt goal allows no step rather than only staying open; and R24 records the widening as accepted. Keeping the three applied passages and skipping the standards pair at Test were the lead's own picks (decision 416).

Test round 2, the closing read with the QA pair, found nothing high. A lead could pass on a goal that reached it in a resume prompt; the C5 test missed a sentence appended to the Stop bullet; the C6 list missed other spellings; two index lines and a log sentence were stale. Past the two-round limit, a round 3 was the lead's own pick (decision 428). The owner, shown the lead's picks on the threat-model questions, answered that at level 3 they don't want to stop for this (decision 432, relayed): the applied edits stand, a step log showing when the owner was away is accepted as R69, and R24's accepted class names a forged lead's non-security pick.

Test round 3, past the limit, found nothing high: the security pair released the push, and integrity-lens noted one low. Meanwhile the owner asked for every pact rule on relayed words and lead sessions to be removed (decision 437, #443), and the lead held the merge (decision 438). The lead's settled wording, its own pick, made a goal the owner's words with no channel named. One more commit dropped the relay and lead lines: the resume-prompt rules, a lead's picks and their review-procedure lines, and the lead terms in the threat model's passages. Trade-offs under a goal all come to the owner again, and R24's widening was narrowed to a forged lead's start prompt or message carrying words that read as the owner's.

Test round 4 found nothing high. The owner's words, relayed (decision 451), accepted every remaining risk entry and threat-model edit as recommended, so R24 now names any prompt or message a session reads, R69 stands, and the step log names the goal's way in by its kind.
